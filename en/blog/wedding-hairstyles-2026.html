Wedding hairstyles 2026: trends for the bride and groom

Wedding hair in 2026 should look like you, just on your best day. Updos are getting softer, waves glossier, and the groom is swapping stiff wax for natural texture.

By Hot Cut · 4 October 2026

The bride: soft, considered and personal

The big shift in 2026 is away from the tight and overstyled. The bride's hair should look considered, but still be comfortable to wear all day. The inspiration comes from romantic, old-fashioned hairstyles, but with lived-in texture and natural movement.

The soft updo

The updo is back in a big way: softly shaped buns, modern French twists and a low, romantic chignon at the nape with loose strands around the face. It holds its shape through the ceremony and looks good in photos, from the front and the back.

Half up, half down

Still a favourite, because it gives you the best of both worlds: structure for the ceremony and movement for the party. We are seeing lifted crowns, loose twists, small braids and natural waves.

Glossy, loose waves

Waves are becoming more fluid and natural, with shine rather than tight curls. They particularly suit medium and long hair, and they are a perfect match for a simple, Nordic wedding.

The bouncy blowout

Inspired by the red carpet: healthy, shiny hair with soft volume and waves that frame the face. Timeless and modern at the same time.

Accessories: from flowers to jewellery

The boho flower crown is taking a step back in favour of more sculptural hairpins, pearls and metal details. Fresh flowers placed discreetly in an updo still go really well with the Nordic style, though.

The groom: natural texture rather than wax

The groom's hair is getting more relaxed too. Stiff, shiny styles are being replaced by matte finishes and natural texture.

  • Textured crop: short sides and texture on top. Modern, easy and smart with a suit.
  • Soft fade: a subtle transition at the sides that looks sharp without feeling severe.
  • Textured quiff: lift at the front and a little length on top. A classic with a modern twist.
  • Longer hair and waves: for the groom who has grown his hair. Perfect for an outdoor wedding.

A good rule of thumb is to get your hair cut one to two weeks before the wedding, so the cut has time to settle. Don't forget the beard either.

How to plan your wedding hair

  • Book a trial updo in good time, so we can find the look together before the big day.
  • Bring photos of the dress, the accessories and your inspiration.
  • Think about make-up too, so your hair and make-up work together. We can do your make-up on the same day.
  • Book early, especially if your wedding is in the busy summer season.
