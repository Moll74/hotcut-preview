Men's haircuts autumn 2026: more texture, more length

The tight, freshly cut fade has some competition. This autumn, men's hair is about texture, a bit more length and a look that works without half an hour in front of the mirror.

By Hot Cut · 4 October 2026

From tight fade to natural fall

In recent years, many men's cuts have been short, sharp and heavily styled. That is now changing. The hairdressers behind Euroman's autumn guide point to more length at the sides and the nape, more movement and a softer look. International barbers say the same: matte finishes and soft transitions are replacing shiny wax.

That doesn't mean the fade is dead. It has simply become softer, and it is more often used as a subtle transition than as the main feature of the cut.

Four cuts we're seeing right now

Textured crop

Short at the sides, texture on top and a light fringe. It is one of the most requested cuts, because it looks considered and almost styles itself with a little matte paste.

Warrior cut

More fullness and texture on top, lifted away from the forehead, and closer at the sides and nape. The inspiration is clear: Brad Pitt in Fight Club and David Beckham around the 2006 World Cup.

Mod cut with length at the nape

Longer hair all the way round, with a sweep at the nape and layers that give movement. This cut suits you if you want to grow your hair without going through a messy in-between stage.

Buzz cut

The very short clipper cut is back, partly after Erling Haaland's change of style. The modern version often has small differences in length or a soft fade, so it doesn't look flat.

Which cut suits you?

It depends on your face shape, your hair type and how much time you want to spend in the morning. A few rules of thumb from the chair:

  • Round face: texture on top and shorter sides give more definition.
  • Square face: a classic side parting or slightly longer hair with texture.
  • Thin hair: shorter with texture, such as a crop or a fade, makes the hair look fuller.

Many people bring a video or a photo. That's a good start, and we always adapt it to your hair so it works at home too.

How to keep the shapeGet a cut every 6–8 weeks. A fade often needs a little more frequent upkeep. Choose matte products rather than shiny gel, and blow-dry your hair in the direction you want it to sit.
